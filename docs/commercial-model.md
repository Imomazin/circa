# Commercial model

How Circa frames the **commercial value** of a circular-economy opportunity — the product thesis, and the commercial levers it makes visible. This is a product/analytical document, not a description of any commercial arrangement between the partners.

> No confidential partnership terms, revenue arrangements or private documents are included here or anywhere in this public repository.

## Thesis

Circular-economy moves are often justified environmentally but stall commercially because the **commercial case is hard to see**. Circa's thesis is that the commercial value is real and can be made legible: circular models frequently improve **resilience** (less exposure to volatile virgin-material markets), unlock **recurring revenue** (service, subscription, product-as-a-service), and create **new margin** (refurbishment, remanufacturing, take-back) — but they also carry **capital, evidence and execution risk** that must be weighed.

Circa exists to answer: **does this circular business opportunity make commercial sense?** — and to show the working.

## Circular models supported

Reuse, Repair, Refurbishment, Remanufacturing, Take-back, Leasing, Subscription, Product-as-a-Service, Sharing, Closed-loop material recovery, Waste-to-value, Lifetime extension. A business may pursue more than one.

## Commercial logic by model (illustrative)

- **Refurbishment / Remanufacturing / Repair** — new margin from recovered value; deeper customer relationships via take-back; watch supplier concentration of reclaimable stock and repair-workforce build.
- **Product-as-a-Service / Leasing / Subscription** — converts one-off sales into recurring, higher-margin revenue and improves asset utilisation; needs upfront capital and strong retention/unit economics.
- **Closed-loop material recovery / Waste-to-value** — hedges virgin-material and disposal-cost exposure; can be capital-heavy with thin margins, so the investment case hinges on scale and evidence.
- **Reuse / Lifetime extension / Sharing** — often low-capital and resilience-accretive; scale and unit economics are the constraint.

## From model to money

The scenario modeller expresses each model's commercial impact as changes to a business's baseline P&L (revenue, material/energy/labour cost, opex, maintenance), plus a capital ask, a recurring-revenue share, a retention assumption and a residual value. It then reports EBITDA, EBITDA uplift, gross margin, cash requirement and payback across baseline, circular base, upside and downside cases — the numbers a commercial decision actually turns on.
